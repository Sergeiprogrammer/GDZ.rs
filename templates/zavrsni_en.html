Below are the basic pieces of information about the final exam—what it is, how to prepare, how to choose the third test, what to do if you don't understand something, as well as recommendations for using additional sources of knowledge.

What is the Final Exam?

  • The final exam is a basic test taken at the end of primary school. Based on the results, the student can choose which secondary school to attend: a vocational school or a gymnasium.
  • The student receives three tests: Serbian language, mathematics, and one elective test.
  • The elective test may be in geography, chemistry, history, physics, or biology.
  • Based on the final exam results and overall academic performance, the student is ranked for admission to the desired secondary school. Some schools may have additional tests, so find out their requirements in advance.
  • Tests are usually held at the end of the school year and show how well the student has mastered the school curriculum.
  • A student can earn a maximum of 14 points on the Serbian language test, 14 points in mathematics, and 12 points on the elective test. That's a total of 40 points from exams, plus up to 60 points from overall school success, for a maximum of 100 points.

How to Prepare for the Final Exam?

  • Start preparing at the beginning of the school year, gradually and without delay.
  • It is important to understand the material, not just memorize it. Previous years' tests may differ from this year's, so pay attention to key concepts.
  • Ask teachers and your homeroom teacher for advice and information.
  • If possible, take lessons with a tutor or seek help where you are uncertain.
  • Study regularly and systematically, and create a study schedule.

How to Choose the Third Test and Prepare for the Others?

  • Choose the third test based on the subject you are most comfortable with and where you can get additional help.
  • For example, if you have support in biology (e.g., a grandmother who knows biology well), it might be a good choice.
  • Practice with past exams. This helps you understand the test structure and types of questions.
  • Take breaks. Don't overload yourself; rest helps with concentration and memory.
  • Don't forget to rest. Adequate sleep and fresh air improve energy and concentration.

What if I'm Afraid of the Final Exam?

  • Start on time. Don't leave preparation until the last minute. Break the material into smaller segments and study gradually.
  • Consider the bigger picture. It's not the end of the world if you don't write it perfectly. You'll have many chances in the future. You can change direction, study online, or gain practical experience later in life.
  • Use various sources. Read textbooks, watch videos, take practice tests, and review formulas.
  • Practice old problems. This helps you get familiar with the question types.
  • Take regular breaks. Avoid overload; rest improves focus.
  • Don't neglect rest. Adequate sleep and outdoor activities reduce stress.
